## Korea Has the Most Aggressive
Quantum Technology Plan
on Earth.

<h4><strong>South Korea · National Quantum Strategy · 2025–2035 · The Most Aggressive Plan on Earth</strong></h4><h3>Korea Has the Most Aggressive<br />Quantum Technology Plan<br />on Earth.<br />$2.3 billion. A dedicated Quantum Act. Samsung, LG, SK Telecom, Hyundai - all in. 1,000-qubit target. Indigenous PQC algorithms. GPS-free military navigation. League of Legends quantum literacy. Five pillars. One decade.</h3><p>South Korea passed dedicated quantum legislation, launched a Quantum Strategy Committee, committed a $2.3 billion national plan, ran its own domestic PQC competition, deployed a 100-qubit hybrid quantum-classical computer, and got Samsung, LG, SK Telecom, and Hyundai all simultaneously investing in quantum. No other country has moved this fast across this many dimensions simultaneously.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*y-xFhwxOmlJ0WVk15v-2MA.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1004/1*2IB4f4M1yb1dqALKC3rVaw.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*8MSLut-B4mDXzW1fdn0Q8w.png" /></figure><p><strong>I </strong>have written in this series about Poland’s 1,770-kilometre quantum-secured defence network, India’s BISAG-N and QNu Labs pact combining indigenous software and hardware, and the Poland-Germany-Czech Republic cross-border quantum corridor under construction. Each of those is a significant national programme. None of them is doing what South Korea is doing. South Korea has passed dedicated quantum legislation, launched a national strategy committee at the presidential level, committed $2.3 billion across a ten-year plan, ran its own full domestic post-quantum cryptography competition, deployed its first hybrid quantum-classical computer, and simultaneously mobilised Samsung, LG Electronics, SK Telecom, Hyundai, and KT the industrial backbone of the entire Korean economy all moving toward quantum at the same time. No other country is moving this fast across this many dimensions simultaneously. Here is what Korea is actually building.</p><p>The starting point is the Quantum Science and Technology Act legislation that Korea passed to create the legal and institutional framework for its quantum strategy. The law established the Quantum Strategy Committee, which held its first meeting in March 2025. At that meeting, the committee announced a dedicated quantum investment fund and set out technical targets that are as specific as they are ambitious: 1,000-qubit quantum computers, quantum repeaters for long-distance quantum key distribution, and GPS-free quantum navigation sensors for defence. The legislation also mandated structured quantum education programmes across the tertiary system and created the framework for what the government calls a “mission-based” approach to R&amp;D defined outcomes, sector-specific applications, clear timelines.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/931/1*9AdoeicOh5dngbWo8KXkRA.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/908/1*CrTIen7h6GhiQUiVAU6ekw.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/912/1*bvyYMh9_GhwPcqn1DBz3ew.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/905/1*BMOK7xyPdSz9gcXDh7jlWQ.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/908/1*f564JMlFHc9Ah0gMiZpoww.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/910/1*Su8piSf6GLO830ZNTghPQA.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*VN9FF1eQ9_v6WWr_ZvZMyQ.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/935/1*-VlRNlIKp_l2UxBGqELTqg.png" /></figure><p>Government strategy papers are common. Government strategy papers backed by the simultaneous real-money commitment of the country’s largest industrial conglomerates are different in kind. Korea’s quantum programme has both.</p><p>Samsung Electronics and Samsung SDS are active in post-quantum cryptography and quantum-relevant semiconductor manufacturing. Samsung SDS co-developed AIMer the algorithm selected in Korea’s own national PQC competition with KAIST. This is not a research partnership. It is a company with one of the world’s largest semiconductor manufacturing operations developing the cryptographic standard that its own products will be required to implement as Korean government mandates take effect. The alignment between Samsung’s commercial interest and the national cryptographic agenda is structural, not incidental.</p><p>SK Telecom’s commitment goes further than most Western telecom companies have come. SKT has integrated QKD into its commercial networks and its AI data centres merging quantum security with AI infrastructure in a single deployable product. In 2025, SKT formalised a deep equity relationship with IonQ by exchanging its ID Quantique shares for a stake in the US quantum computing company creating a financial alignment between the world’s most commercially advanced trapped-ion quantum system maker and Korea’s largest wireless carrier. SKT is explicitly targeting the convergence of quantum computing and AI recognising that the two technologies will need each other, and positioning to be the infrastructure layer when that convergence arrives.</p><p>LG Electronics is collaborating with KRISS on neutral atom QPU development. Hyundai Motor Group has a quantum computing partnership with IonQ. KT is involved in quantum communication network infrastructure. The Busan Metropolitan Government signed an MoU with IonQ to advance quantum technology in Korea’s second-largest city. The coverage of Korea’s industrial base by quantum investment is not comprehensive there are gaps, and some experts have noted that government spending still lags behind the US, China, and the UK in absolute terms but the breadth across conglomerates is matched nowhere else.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/913/1*dNQEYVsMhzRdiB4WIc4FnQ.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/934/1*vnKVddn_my2KsulIxGAH_g.png" /></figure><p>This is the detail that got me. Korea’s quantum strategy includes using League of Legends as a public engagement tool for quantum literacy. For readers who need context: League of Legends was made by Riot Games but is played at a higher rate in South Korea than almost anywhere else on Earth. Korean professional LoL players are internationally recognised as among the best in the world. The game is genuinely woven into youth culture in a way that very few other things are.</p><p>Using it as a touchpoint for quantum normalisation is not gimmicky it is the most rational public engagement strategy available. If the goal is to train 2,500 quantum researchers starting from a small base, the pipeline has to begin long before university. It has to begin at the level where young Koreans are making decisions about what to study, what to care about, what they imagine their professional futures looking like. A quantum strategy that can only reach people who are already interested in physics is not a talent strategy. A quantum strategy that reaches people through what they are actually doing which in Korea, for a significant portion of the youth population, includes League of Legends is one that can actually change the pipeline at scale.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*nlC1YZGNaktnyN1f1et3fw.png" /></figure><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/931/1*p73KWg7IevFilEihMUyIvw.png" /></figure><p>The picture is not uniformly positive, and a comprehensive account requires the caveats. The most significant one is the gap between stated ambition and current funding scale. While Korea increased its quantum R&amp;D budget by 51% from 2024 to 2025 from $140 million to approximately $250 million this remains significantly below the absolute investment levels of the United States, China, and the United Kingdom. Korea’s own experts are frank about this. Han Sang-wook, President of the Korea Quantum Information Science Association, stated publicly that the investment scale “remains a concern” relative to global competitors, and that the government “should send a clear message to encourage large corporations to invest more aggressively.” The Ministry of Science and ICT acknowledged at a press briefing in 2025 that no additional supplementary budget would be allocated despite this criticism.</p><p>The domestic hardware capability also still lags behind the frontier. KRISS’s 20-qubit domestic processor is impressive as an indigenous achievement but is far from the most capable quantum hardware deployed in Korea that is currently the IonQ Tempo 100 system in HANKANG, which is American-built. Korea’s quantum hardware strategy involves three parallel architecture programmes superconducting, neutral atom, photonic which is the right approach for avoiding lock-in but means resources are spread across multiple development tracks rather than concentrated. The 1,000-qubit target is a 2030 goal; getting there from 20 qubits of domestic capability in four years would be one of the fastest hardware scaling programmes in the world, and the intermediate steps will be challenging.</p><p>These are real limitations. They exist within a programme that is, by the composite measure of legislative commitment, corporate mobilisation, cultural integration, cryptographic sovereignty, and the specificity and breadth of its technical targets, the most comprehensive national quantum strategy currently operating anywhere. The comparison is not with the ideal. It is with what other countries are actually doing.</p><figure><img alt="" src="https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/928/1*-1rKrUgI18LujvnaYPTr4A.png" /></figure><p>I study information technology in Puducherry. Korea’s semiconductor industry is woven into the hardware I use every day the chips in the device I am writing this on are likely to have Korean origins somewhere in the supply chain. Samsung is not an abstract national programme to me. It is the manufacturing reality of the technology ecosystem I inhabit as a student and that I will inhabit as a professional.</p><p>What Korea is doing with quantum is the same thing it did with semiconductors in the 1980s and 1990s: identifying a technology that will be foundational to the next generation of everything, committing national resources to building domestic capability before dependence on foreign supply chains becomes irreversible, and mobilising the industrial conglomerates to treat it as a strategic priority rather than an optional investment. The chaebols Samsung, LG, SK, Hyundai, KT are not moving toward quantum because they think it is interesting. They are moving because they understand what happened to countries that did not build semiconductor capability when they had the chance.</p><p>India’s quantum strategy is real and funded the National Quantum Mission with its ₹6,003 crore budget. Poland’s is operational with 1,770 kilometres of deployed QKD fiber. Korea’s is something different in scope: a legislative act, a presidential-level strategy committee, four indigenous cryptographic algorithms, Samsung’s full cryptographic stack, SK Telecom’s QKD-AI convergence, a 100-qubit hybrid computing environment, five parallel hardware architecture programmes, seven times the talent pool in a decade, and League of Legends as a quantum literacy vector. That is not a plan that reads like a plan. That is a plan that reads like a country that has decided to win.</p><p><em>Sources: Quantum World Congress 2025, “South Korea Accelerates National Quantum Strategy With New Five-Year Plan” (December 2025) · The Quantum Insider, “Building Quantum Literacy: South Korea’s Strategic Integration of Science, Society, and Sovereignty” (June 2025) · postquantum.com, “Quantum Technologies and Quantum Computing in South Korea” · The Quantum Insider, “South Korea Launches Quantum Strategy Committee” (March 2025) · IonQ / KISTI, “IonQ and KISTI Finalize Agreement to Deliver 100-Qubit Quantum System in South Korea” (December 23, 2025) · IonQ / SK Telecom / ID Quantique merger announcement (February 26, 2025) · National Quantum Strategy quantuminkorea.org · Quantum World Tour: South Korea (January 28, 2026) · Samsung SDS / KAIST AIMer KpqC selection.</em></p><h3>A message from our Founder</h3><p>Hey, <a href="https://linkedin.com/in/sunilsandhu">Sunil</a> here.
